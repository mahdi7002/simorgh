## What these numbers do and do not show

- Queries are **derived from the database itself**; they measure retrieval and abstention behaviour, not accuracy on
  arbitrary user input. A verse attributed to the wrong poet inside the source database would count as correct.
- One seed, one sample of 300 verses, one machine.
- The QA set (n=10) is a starter set; no general claim about LLM factual accuracy should be made from it.
  In it, the only fabricated answer was a wrong author for a classical work; two other misses were honest "I don't know".
- Robustness to misspelling is limited: with one dropped letter the system answers only ~35% of queries, by design
  it abstains rather than guess (0 wrong answers among the 106 it did give).
- **Defect found by this evaluation and fixed (commit `e5750c7`).** In the first run, one of 288 answered "middle 4 words"
  queries was wrong: «و روشن دل و» (true: Nizami «قوی رای و روشن دل و سرفراز») was attributed to Ferdowsi, because the first
  8 hits of that stock phrase all belonged to Ferdowsi and the lookup treated a full result window as proof of uniqueness.
  Fix: a phrase with more than k occurrences is no longer certified to a single poet (status "uncertain").
  After the fix the same sample gives 287 answered / 287 correct and 13 abstentions (true poet among the candidates in 12 of 13).
- **Caveat:** the defect was found and fixed on this same sample (seed 7), so the post-fix 287/287 is not an independent
  estimate. Report results on fresh seeds (see "Held-out check" below) alongside it.

Notes
- F uses the dev seed (7). The LLM-alone numbers do not depend on any tuning, but the final paper should repeat F on held-out seeds (11, 23, 37).
- The lookup's queries come from the database itself (EASY setting); the LLM was given no retrieval. The comparison shows what retrieval adds on this task, not how a user-facing assistant performs on arbitrary questions.
- The model never said "I don't know" on verse attribution (0/100), whereas the system abstains when it cannot certify a poet.
- Sample sizes in D are tiny (8 and 10 items); no general claim should be made from them.
